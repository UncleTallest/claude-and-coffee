# Why Communication Guides?

## The Problem

Most people discover they need a Communication Guide after getting annoyed at Claude for the third time in a row.

Classic triggers:

- Claude keeps asking too many questions at once
- It's being relentlessly chipper when you just want to get something done
- It keeps adding caveats you didn't ask for
- You're re-explaining the same preferences every single session

## What a Communication Guide Does

A Communication Guide is a cheat sheet you give Claude about how you specifically communicate - not what you want help with, but how your brain works and what makes interactions smooth versus frustrating.

Think of it as calibration. Without it, every session starts cold. Claude has to guess what you want, how you think, and what annoys you. With it, Claude arrives already tuned to your frequency.

## What Goes In One

### How you think and talk

- Do you think out loud and sharpen as you go, or come in with fully formed asks?
- Do you answer in bullet lists?
- Do you use profanity as punctuation?
- Do you want blunt feedback or a softer frame?

### What works

Specific things that actually land:

- "Show me a draft rather than describing what a draft could say"
- "Give me options when I'm stuck on a word"
- "Name the tradeoff when there are two valid paths"

### What doesn't work

The stuff that makes you want to close the tab:

- Affirmations
- Too many questions
- A specific punctuation mark you hate (em-dashes are a common casualty)
- Unnecessary explanations of obvious things

### Context that changes calibration

- Your background
- Your expertise level in relevant areas
- How to read your emotional register accurately

## Structure

Prose or headers with short sections both work fine. The goal is something Claude can absorb quickly and actually use - not a manifesto.

If it's so long that Claude has to summarize it to itself, it's probably too long.

## The Real Trick: Specificity

"Be direct" is almost useless.

"Don't add affirmations before answering and don't ask more than one question at a time" is actually actionable.

The more specific you are, the more Claude can actually adjust.
